# Gleam Auto Detailing — Photography & Imagery Art Direction

*Phase 4 of 5 — Shot list and grading direction for shoot or curated imagery. References the Phase 2 colour palette.*

---

## Lighting Direction

- **Golden hour (primary):** shoot exterior hero and lifestyle content in the 45 minutes after sunrise or before sunset, when Miami light turns warm and low — this is the light that makes Causeway Gold and chrome reflections read correctly on camera.
- **Harsh midday contrast (detail shots only):** intentionally shoot swirl-mark and correction detail shots at midday, when direct overhead sun exposes every imperfection — this is the light a client's own eye would use to judge the work, so the proof has to hold up under it.
- **Studio lighting (interior close-ups):** soft box, diffused, colour-temperature matched to daylight (5000–5600K) for leather, stitching, and trim shots, avoiding the yellow cast of typical garage lighting.

## Camera Angles

- **Hero shots:** low angle, slightly below the vehicle's beltline, shooting up along the length of the car — conveys presence and scale without needing a supercar backdrop.
- **Detail/macro shots:** water beads on a hood shot at a raking, near-parallel angle to the paint surface, so the camera captures the bead's reflection and depth rather than a flat top-down view.
- **Reflection depth shots:** a straight-on panel shot capturing a recognizable reflection (a palm frond, a building line) in freshly coated paint — proof of correction told through what the paint reflects, not just its shine.
- **Interior macro:** 45-degree close-up on stitching and leather grain, shallow depth of field, one seam in crisp focus with the rest falling into soft blur.

## Miami-Specific Backgrounds & Settings

- Pastel Art Deco facades of South Beach for lifestyle/hero shots (used sparingly — one or two hero images, not the whole gallery).
- Dappled palm-frond shadow patterns cast directly onto a freshly polished hood — ties the correction result to a specifically Miami light condition.
- Marina or causeway backdrop at golden hour for founder/handover moments.
- Studio bay interior (exposed brick or polished concrete, warm task lighting) for process shots — grounds the brand in real craft, not just outdoor glamour.

## The Human Element

- Hands mid-motion with a microfiber cloth or foam applicator, caught in a natural wiping arc rather than posed holding-still.
- Founder inspecting a panel under a raking LED light, genuine concentration, not looking at camera.
- Client handover: candid reaction caught the moment they see the finished car — a hand to the mouth, a low whistle, a phone immediately coming out to take photos. Never staged handshakes.
- A technician's forearm and gloved hand applying ceramic coating in a slow, deliberate pass — communicates care and process, not speed.

## Shot List (minimum 8 distinct types)

1. Hero — low-angle exterior with water beading, golden hour.
2. Detail macro — water bead raking-light close-up.
3. Reflection depth — recognizable environment reflected in corrected paint.
4. Interior macro — stitching/leather close-up, shallow depth of field.
5. Process — hands with microfiber cloth mid-motion.
6. Process — founder inspecting panel under raking LED light.
7. Handover — candid client reaction at reveal.
8. Environment — palm shadow pattern across a freshly polished hood.
9. Environment — studio bay wide shot showing real tools and workspace.
10. Lifestyle — vehicle framed against Art Deco or marina backdrop at golden hour (used sparingly, 1–2 images max).

## Colour Grading & Filter Direction

Match the Phase 2 palette: warm highlights pulled toward Sun Chrome and Causeway Gold, shadows deepened toward Deep Bay rather than crushed to pure black, mid-contrast (not the flattened look of a heavy preset filter). Skin tones stay natural and un-warmed even when the surrounding scene carries a golden cast, so handover/reaction shots don't look artificially tanned. No teal-orange grading — the palette discipline from the visual identity applies to photography too: chrome and gold accents, not beach-postcard color pop.
